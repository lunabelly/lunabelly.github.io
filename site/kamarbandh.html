Kamarbandh guide

What is a kamarbandh, and how do you wear one?

A short, practical guide from a small Birmingham shop that sells waist jewellery and nothing else. It covers what a kamarbandh is, how to wear it, how to choose one for a wedding, and how buying from us works.

What is a kamarbandh?

A kamarbandh is jewellery worn around the waist, traditionally with a saree or a lehenga. The name joins two words, kamar (waist) and bandh (to tie or fasten). You will also see it spelled kamarband, and a fine waist chain is often called a kardhani. In South India the broad belt style is known as an oddiyanam, or vaddanam in Telugu.

It can be a single fine chain, several layered strands, or a broad belt with a centrepiece. The fine styles are often called waist chains or belly chains, and many people wear them with everyday clothes as well as traditional outfits. Our guide to the difference between a kamarbandh, an oddiyanam, a waist chain and a belly chain goes into more detail.

How to wear a kamarbandh

With a lehenga, wear it at the natural waist, where the skirt band sits. It frames the gap between blouse and skirt, and this is where a bold pendant looks its best.

With a saree, rest it lower on the hips, just below the navel, with the centrepiece lined up over the pleats. A broad oddiyanam-style belt goes over the saree and helps hold the pleats in place. Our step-by-step article on how to wear a kamarbandh with a saree covers the drape and the most common mistake.

With everyday clothes, a single fine chain worn low on the hips looks good with jeans, a cropped top or a summer dress. See how to wear a waist chain with jeans.

Whatever you wear it with, put it on last so drops and bells do not catch on fine fabric. Measure around your body where you want it to sit, not your jeans size, because a kamarbandh usually sits higher or lower than a waistband. Choosing a kamarbandh for an Indian wedding

There is no single best kamarbandh. The right one depends on the outfit and on you. A few things help:

  • Match the weight of the embroidery. Heavy zari, temple work or a kundan necklace set can take a broad belt or a large pendant. Lighter work suits pearl strands or a fine chain.
  • Match the metal and style of your other jewellery. An antique or temple finish sits well with older-gold and temple sets. Kundan pairs with kundan and polki.
  • Think about comfort. You will wear it for many hours, through the ceremony, photographs and dancing. Every product page lists the weight in grams, so you can compare.
  • Choose it with your necklace, not after. It is often the last piece chosen and the one that finishes the look.
